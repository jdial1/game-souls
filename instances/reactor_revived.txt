## Soul Instance: Reactor Revived

**Status:** Translated (Phases 0–4 reconstructed from the build, then revisited in the designer's Soul Interview; no playtest is recorded, so Phases 5–6 are open; the Feature Gate is running; a Phase 8 audit was run on the build and does not pass yet)
**Lead soul:** souls/containment.txt (Path: Adopt)
**Last milestone review:** 27 September 2026, on the build at reactor-revived commit a7c0215 (the Drift Audit below). Phases 0, 1 and 4 revisited the same week through the designer's Soul Interview (docs/soul-interview.md in the game's repository).

*Reactor Revived is a real game in development: a zero-dependency rewrite of Reactor Knockoff for Android phones, and the source game of Containment. The soul was drawn from this game, so its litmus was written to describe features that already existed, which is the definition of pitfalls/soul_written_last.txt. This instance turns that caption back into a constraint: it reconstructs Phases 0–4 from the build and the game's soul report (docs/soul.md in its repository), then holds the build to them with a Feature Gate and a drift audit that can fail, and did. Paths to the game's files are in its repository. Nothing here is claimed about players, because no playtest is recorded.*

### Seed Card (Phase 0)

| Verb | What the player literally does | Kinetic profile (bands, if real-time) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Place a part | Tap an empty tile with a part selected, or drag to paint a row | Not a real-time verb: instant, and refunded if sold, so never committed |
| Inspect, then sell, move, or replace | Tap a placed part for its sheet; every action on a placed part lives there | Not a real-time verb: instant; a sale refunds the price less fuel used |
| Buy | Buy an upgrade, a doctrine side, or research with Exotic Particles | Not a real-time verb: a menu choice |

The reactor runs in real time around these verbs, but no verb is performed by reflex. Selling power and venting heat by tapping the bars is the Manual Tax (components/automation.txt), not a core verb: the first upgrades retire it.

Unpausing is the reactor's on switch (the designer's word, interview 1.1): the moment a board built by these verbs is set running.

* **The fortieth minute:** "A feeling of accomplishment pulled from confusing and uncertainty": the struggle first, then watching it flow together, then confirming it holds and leaving it, confident it is stable (interview 1.1, 6.3).
* **The next-day story:** "I figured out the accelerator layout," told with excitement (interview 1.4).
* **Setting (settled in the interview):** Harrow Station, an old Soviet-built reactor in an English valley of fog and overcast. A control room left in clean order over a core that needs replacing. A valley that may be run by humanoid robots, waiting on an approval nobody is left to give, and asking only for more. Story is told only in letters and log entries (interview 2-6).

*The fortieth minute and the next-day story were first inferred from the build. The designer replaced both, and settled the setting, in the Soul Interview (docs/soul-interview.md). The inferred versions are in the Soul Ledger.*

*Integration check:* Delete Harrow Station and every verb still produces the feeling; the setting does no work a mechanic should. It supplies what the build lacked, by the designer's own verdict: a why (interview 6.5). It reaches the player only through the log, letters, names, voice, materials and sound, never through a scene.

### Soul Choice (Phase 1)

* **Path:** Adopt. Containment's philosophy sentence was written from this game and describes the seed with no edits. Loathing lends furniture (records, trophies, restriction runs taken like ascension paths, and the fair-play contract) but leads no axis and explains no part of the seed Containment doesn't, so this is not a Blend; the borrowings are entered as components and a house rule.
* **Instance philosophy (the Soul Sentence, interview 7.1):** "Paid to start a Soviet reactor nobody else could, in an English fog, you learn it alone until it holds. The valley asks for more."
  Still a Member of Containment: "until it holds" is the soul. What it adds is the world the holding is for. It replaces the philosophy first inferred from the build ("A reactor small enough to hold in one hand, and good enough to put in your pocket"); see the Soul Ledger.
* **Blend leaders:** not applicable.

**Psychological Target** (copied from Containment; one row changed)

| Motivation Pair | Level | Why | Changed? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Action | Low | Nothing is performed by reflex; the reactor keeps time, the player doesn't. | No |
| Social | Low | Designs travel as codes; play is solitary and offline. | No |
| Mastery | High | Strategy: ratios, geometry, and a conserved ledger. | No |
| Achievement | High | Marks, Mark I records, restriction-run times, and a trophy case. | No |
| Immersion | Medium | The machine is still the point, and the world now gives it a why: letters and log entries, optional and never interrupting play. | Yes: the designer's verdict that the build "lacks the why" (interview 6.5), bounded in 6.3. |
| Creativity | High | Design: a layout that holds is the player's own, and it travels as text. | No |

**Design note:** Two groups will ask for things that work against this game. Immersion players will ask for Harrow Station to grow into a story told on screen; story lives only in letters and log entries, optional, and never interrupts play. Phone idle players, and the store around them, will ask for what the genre expects: notifications that call them back, daily rewards, ads, and offline earnings that pile up while they are away. Each of those is on the banned list.

### Axis Positions (Phase 2)

| Axis | Lead Soul | This Game | Match / Bend / Break |

| Information (rules / state) | Total (state) / Observed (consequence) | Rules: every part's rule is stated on its sheet and in the parts guide, except how hard touching cells pulse, which is observed. | Authorship | Player-Authored Build | "My Mark I": layout codes, save states, modules | Match |
| Power | Optimization | Ratios, geometry, upgrades, doctrine sides | Match |
| Tone | Watchful Calm | The hum steadies when a board holds and beats while heat climbs; near the limit the reactor is the loudest thing in the game | Match |

**Tensions, resolved:**
* **Total + shallow systems** (axes/information.txt): a visible board can be solved. The depth is combinatorial (thirteen part kinds with their own neighbour rules on 96 tiles), and doctrine sides are there to move the optimum between runs. The watch is pitfalls/checkerboard_forever.txt, because most of the 72 upgrades only scale a number.
* **Costly failure + a save file** (named by no axis file; Containment's pitfalls/backdoor_rewind.txt): a meltdown is only Costly if nothing brings the board back. Import is refused in Hardcore and marked *Restored* elsewhere. Android's cloud backup was a second way back; see the Drift Audit.
* **Glance-length sessions + trusting_the_player** (components/trusting_the_player.txt, "short sessions for casual audiences"): a phone is picked up for a minute at a time, and there is no time to wander. Resolved by letting the reactor keep time instead of the player: it runs on every page, and time away is banked as Time Flux to spend on the player's say. The discovery happens across many short sessions, not inside one.
* **What stops the clock** (pitfalls/pause_loophole.txt, by analogy): Pause, the planner, and leaving the app stop the real board; every other page lets it run. Leaving banks the time; Pause and the planner don't, so stopping the clock never earns anything.

| Anything that calls the player back or punishes absence: notifications, daily rewards, streaks, spoilage, offline earnings that expire | This instance (house rule 2) | The phone idle genre expects all of them. Here a held machine is one you are allowed to leave. |

The workbench is marked Core here, where docs/soul.md has it Supporting: it is the largest system in the build (planner, forecast, codes, save states, the module designer), and a Supporting component that eats the budget is a sign that either the bill or the soul is wrong. Here it is the bill. Containment's Pillar 4 is the workbench.

**Relationship conflicts, resolved:**
* **scarcity_economy against an Inflationary topology:** the scarce thing is tiles, not the money that inflates, and rebirth is the money sink.
* **scarcity_economy against synergy_engines late in a run:** synergy is geometric, so every combo spends the tiles that stay scarce.
* **workbench and legible_failure against outcome randomness:** the only roll is the fractional Exotic Particle count, a reward, and the forecast seeds it.
* **workbench against Terminal failure:** failure is Costly; Hardcore removes the planner and codes for players who want the stakes whole.
* **automation against automating decisions:** rebuys and selling are chores with one right answer. Heat Control Operator is off until the player turns it on.
* **audio_information against a Total UI, and without a visual twin:** the hum reports the trend, which the screen shows only as the held column and Mark II; every cue has that twin, so the sound can be off.
* **audio_information's positional audio requirement:** not met, and not needed. The reactor is one source; which part is failing is visual.
* **trusting_the_player against tutorials:** eight cards, six of which wait for the player to act, none of which says where a part goes.

### Economy (Phase 3)

* **Shape:** A Generator (power and money grow without limit with investment), with a Drain inside every board (fuel burns out, reflectors wear, condensators cost money to empty), and rebirth as the sink across runs.
* **The bottleneck:** Tiles. Ninety-six of them, never more.
* **Drains:** fuel rebuys; reflector wear; condensator refills; parts lost to heat; a meltdown's whole board; the module casing's cut of power and particles (75%, down to 40% with Casing Tolerances); upgrades at exponential cost; doctrine sets at ten times the last; a reboot's board and money.
* **Why this shape produces the tone:** Money grows but space doesn't, so a richer player is never a safer one; calm has to be built into the arrangement, and a built calm is Watchful Calm.

**House Rules**

* **Inside the Lineage:** Every part, rule, and number comes from IndustrialCraft 2, Reactor Incremental, or Reactor Knockoff. Reactor Incremental is the game being revived; Knockoff is the route it survived by, and the balance is checked against Knockoff running in a browser. A departure is allowed only when it is named and given a reason in the README's "Differences from the original".
  * *Example:* Knockoff's particle accelerator could never make a particle. The fix is listed as a difference, with the reason and the example farm that proves it.
* **Nothing Asked, Nothing Sold:** The game asks nothing of the player but play: no ads, purchases, accounts, network, or notifications, and no clock outside the game that punishes being away. Time away is banked, never spent behind the player's back.
* **One Request, Waiting:** One request at a time, in a silent room. The demand arrives as a line in the log and waits; nothing else speaks. (The designer's, from the Soul Interview, 6.4 and 7.2.)
  * *Example:* A part the player has earned appears in the dock and is filed in the log book. No toast announces it.

### Instance Litmus (Phase 4)

| Check | Question | Correct Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Ledger** | Can the player account for every point of heat the board made this tick, on the rate line or the ledger sheet? | **Yes.** Made equals vented, converted, and held. |
| **The Neighbour** | Would the same parts in a different arrangement on the 12x8 make a different machine? | **Yes.** Geometry is the build. |
| **The Clock** | Is a design judged by how long it holds on the real board, not only by what it makes in a minute? | **Yes.** Mark I is the prestige. |
| **The Workbench** | If players would need an outside tool to design well, does the game ship it, offline? | **Yes.** Planner, forecast, codes, and datasheet are in the box. |
| **The Dice** | Can a part fail, or a board be undone, for a reason the board did not cause? | **No.** Every failure is heat the design made, and it stays made. |
| **The Lineage** | Does it come from IC2, Incremental, or Knockoff, or is it written down as a named difference? | **Yes.** Inside the line, or named. |
| **The Room** | Does it ask the player for anything but the one request waiting: money, data, a connection, a return at a set time, or attention for a second voice? | **No.** Nothing asked, nothing sold, one request waiting. |

The Dice is widened from the soul's row to cover undoing a board: a rewind is the same lie as a random failure, told in the other direction. The Room carries house rules 2 and 3 together, so the litmus stays at seven rows; it replaced The Ask when house rule 3 arrived (see the Soul Ledger). docs/soul.md's extra rows (The Voice, The Chores, The Blindfold) are component checks, and run in the Pitfall Watchlist instead.

**Litmus self-test:** The expected feature list includes the lineage's own features, and six failed. **Knockoff's free trickle** under the heat limit failed The Ledger and was removed. **Board-wide capacitor and plating bonuses** failed The Neighbour and became local. **A peak-power record** as the only prestige number failed The Clock and now sits under Mark I power. **Knockoff's expansion upgrades** failed The Neighbour, whose question is asked on the 12x8 (a board that grows stops fitting the hand), and were cut. **Google Drive saves** failed The Room (then The Ask) and were cut. **Android cloud backup** failed The Dice, because a reinstall restores a board past its meltdown, and was cut in the drift audit (see the Feature Gate Log). House rule 3 was self-tested on the build when it was written: five features add a second voice or a badge to the room, and each has a verdict in the Feature Gate Log.

### Accepted Costs (Phase 8)

* **Tuning ratios, not values:** every change to a part moves the optimum for every shared code. The pinned example balances and the lab-versus-floor test keep it honest; they are also why a balance change is slow.
* **Time as a test input:** a design is proven only by running. Marks take 300 unchanged ticks, and Time Flux and the forecast are what make that bearable.
* **The law is never explained:** some players will melt a packed board, find nothing in the game or the store that tells them why, and leave. The game shows every number and trusts them to read it.
* **Nothing runs in the pocket:** a held machine earns nothing while the app is closed. It banks up to eight hours, to be spent on the player's say.
* **A lost phone is a lost game:** with cloud backup off, only an exported save or a device transfer carries a game forward. Recovering from a meltdown is never one of the things a backup is for.
* **The first hour is by hand:** selling and venting by tapping the bars until the first automation is bought, as every game in the line began.
