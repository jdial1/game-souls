## Soul Instance: infiniteRTS

**Status:** Translated (Phases 0–4 on paper; Phase 6 watchlist drafted; no playtests yet)
**Lead soul:** souls/assembly.txt (Path: Blend with souls/tapestry.txt)
**Last milestone review:** none yet

*infiniteRTS (the menu reads "Red October: Overlord Command") is a real browser RTS in development: one endless, shared, persistent map where every player mines, builds, and claims outposts. This instance was written after a build existed, so it follows an instillation of that build (Step 8 of LLM_SOUL_GUIDE.txt), kept in the game's repository at docs/SOUL.md. The Seed Card is inferred from the build and the designer's answers (the growing machine and the pressing border together; competitive PvP; setting provisional). The designer confirms it before Phase 5.*

---

### Seed Card (Phase 0)

| Verb | What the player literally does | Kinetic profile (bands, if real-time) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stake ground | Walk the hero into an outpost's ring and hold it until it turns (or until a rival's turns neutral) | Deliberate attack, committed hold: progress fills only while the hero stays; a rival in the ring freezes it |
| Route labour | Assign workers to wood, stone, or gold, and decide where they can unload | Menu choice; not real-time |
| Place a building | Put a wall, turret, or base inside the borders | Snappy attack, committed release: instant, paid on the spot |

Gathering by hand is the Manual Tax (components/automation.txt), not a core verb: workers exist to take it away.

* **The fortieth minute:** Calm, because the machine feeds itself and its border was built right, and never quite calm, because a neighbour is measuring that border.
* **The next-day story:** "My stone ran dry at home, so I ran a supply road out to the mountain outposts, and by morning the red player had neutralized two of them."
* **Setting:** A Soviet-industrial frontier. Settled in Phase 4, once the Plan existed to carry it.

*Integration check:* Delete the setting and the verbs still hold. It earned its place when the planned economy's **Plan** became the infinite sink (Pillar 4) and the Commissariat began to speak the ledger. The sci-fi upgrade names were retired.

### Soul Choice (Phase 1)

* **Path:** Blend. Assembly explains the machine: workers, routes, a bottleneck that keeps moving outward, no ending. Tapestry explains the map: ground that only changes hands, borders as the record of play, size that costs something. Neither explains a session alone.
* **Instance philosophy:** "The machine must grow, and every tile it grows onto is a tile someone else wanted."
* **Blend leaders:**
  * **Failure:** Neither. Assembly's Trivial can't survive PvP; Tapestry's Terminal is too much for a persistent world. Costly, in Tapestry's spirit: a lost outpost is the next chapter.
  * **Information:** Tapestry leads. Rules and your own state are Total; a rival's state beyond your sight is Observed through fog.
  * **Authorship:** Assembly leads. Player-Authored Build, with the rival as co-author of the border.
  * **Power:** Assembly leads. Optimization; workers have no wills, so Custodial is dropped.
  * **Tone:** Neither. Watchful Calm (Containment's position), with the danger supplied by rivals instead of heat.
  * **Structure:** Assembly leads. Persistent World, shared and never reset.

**Psychological Target** (copied from Assembly; one row changed)

| Motivation Pair | Level | Why | Changed? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Action | Low | The hero never fights; reflex matters only in a race to an outpost. | No |
| Social | High | Competition: rivals share one map, take ground, and share one board. | Yes: Assembly's co-op neighbours are rivals here, and the rivalry is half the game. |
| Mastery | High | Strategy: labour ratios, routes, and which border can be held. | No |
| Achievement | High | Power: painting the map. | No |
| Immersion | Low | The fiction is thin and provisional. | No |
| Creativity | High | Design: the lattice of outposts, roads, and depots. | No |

**Design note:** Action players will ask for a hero who fights; that moves power from the layout to the hands, and it is banned. Players who want a peaceful factory will meet neighbours who take things. That is the soul, not a flaw to patch.

### Axis Positions (Phase 2)

| Axis | Lead Soul | This Game | Match / Bend / Break |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Failure | Trivial | Costly: buildings and outposts are taken or destroyed and their cost is gone; account, upgrades, and workers survive | Break (ledgered) |
| Information (rules / state) | Total | Total rules; Total state within your own sight; Observed beyond it | Bend (ledgered) |
| Authorship | Player-Authored Build | "What I built, and where they broke it" | Match |
| Power | Optimization | Throughput per worker, ground per upgrade | Match |
| Tone | Cool Focus | Watchful Calm: the machine is quiet because its border was built right, and the neighbour never leaves | Break (ledgered) |
| Structure | Persistent World | One shared map, never reset | Match |

**Tensions, resolved:**
* **Total + shallow systems** (axes/information.txt): a visible economy can be solved. The rival is the depth, and the Moving Bottleneck keeps the solo half from settling.
* **Hidden + Punishing failure:** not triggered. Fog is Observed (what is in range is shown exactly) and failure is Costly.
* **Persistent PvP + absence** (named by no axis file): the world runs while the owner sleeps, so losses happen off-screen. Allowed, because rivalry is the soul, but never unexplained (see Banned Mechanics). Absence cuts both ways: an absent hero holds no ground either. Whether absent players also need a protection window is a Phase 5 question.

### Banned Mechanics (Phase 2)

| Banned Mechanic | Source | Why It Is Banned |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Resources that arrive without a worker (tax ticks, generating walls, collector turrets) | axes/tone.txt (Assembly's killer, translated) | Income from nowhere is the Magic Void (pitfalls/magic_void.txt). *Was in the build; removed.* |
| A loss with no receipt | axes/tone.txt (Watchful Calm's killer, translated) | Calm survives danger; it doesn't survive danger you can't name. *Was in the build; addressed.* |
| Insurance or a rewind that restores what a rival took | axes/failure.txt (Costly) | If the loss can be undone, the border means nothing. |
| A hero who deals damage | This instance (house rule 3) | The genre expects the commander to fight. Here hands stake and buildings fight. |
| A button that decides where labour goes | components/automation.txt | The labour split is the Ratio over Capacity decision. The build's auto-assign makes it for the player. |

| Component | Core / Supporting | This Game's Tuning |

| components/scarcity_economy.txt | Core | Scarcity is ground: depleting nodes inside the borders, and an outpost cap that rises only at a steep price. Never money. |

| components/legible_failure.txt | Core | Two receipts: a stalled worker names its missing input; every loss to a rival is a ledger line with a name, a place, and a time. |
| components/match_integrity.txt | Core (added) | The server decides positions, vision, captures, and deliveries, because ground is the stake. |
| components/systemic_consistency.txt | Supporting | One rule set for every player; every tooltip number is the server's number. |
| components/workbench.txt | Supporting | Show the Rates first (per resource, per depot), then free demolition, then outpost-layout blueprints. |
| components/trusting_the_player.txt | Supporting | Give the lattice rules and the numbers; never suggest where to expand. |
| components/failure_as_content.txt | Supporting (added) | A lost outpost stays on the map as neutral ground with its ledger line: the next thing to retake, and the story of who took it. |

Tapestry's living_world is dropped: rival players already keep the world running.

**Relationship conflicts, resolved:**
* **match_integrity against client-side authority:** The hero spends a movement budget that refills at its legal top speed, and the server corrects any report that outruns it. Each client is sent rival state only inside its own vision, so fog is real in the data, not only on screen (pitfalls/seal_by_convention.txt, by analogy). Outposts stay public.
* **automation against automating decisions:** Standing Orders. The player sets the split (say 3:2:1), and the machine keeps it as workers are bought. The machine keeps a ratio; it never picks one.
* **scarcity_economy against a Generator without drains:** upgrades have linear effects at exponential costs, so buying stops. The Plan is the infinite sink (Pillar 4), capped at a quarter phase per delivery so it never empties the purse in one decision.
* **workbench against outcome randomness:** node sizes are rolled when a chunk is generated, before any decision; turret targeting is deterministic. Nothing is rolled after the player commits.

### Economy (Phase 3)

* **Shape:** A Generator (workers, routes, and upgrades grow output without limit) nested inside a Zero-Sum (outposts only change hands).
* **The bottleneck:** Minable ground. Home nodes run dry, richer nodes lie further out, and each outpost past the cap needs a Base Expansion at four times the last one's price.
* **Drains:** building costs; worker costs that double every ten workers; upgrades at 1.5x per level; Base Expansion at 4x; buildings destroyed by rivals; depleting nodes.
* **Why this shape produces the tone:** The Generator gives Assembly's momentum; the Zero-Sum puts a rival at the edge of every expansion. A working machine watched from a contested border is Watchful Calm.

Tapestry Pillars 1, 2, and 4 are dropped (ledgered). One idea survives from Pillar 4: the ledger writes about people ("the red player neutralized your outpost"), not numbers.

**House Rules**

* **Every Border Is Someone's Border:** Everyone starts near the centre and the best nodes lie further out, so the most valuable ground is always the most contested.
* **The Receipt Arrives Before the Grudge:** Every loss one player inflicts on another is written down with the attacker's name, the place, and the time, and it waits for an absent owner. A player knows who to blame before deciding what to do about it.
  * *Example:* A player logs in to a broken western square. Before the map, the report reads: "The red player's turret destroyed your wall. 03:12 [1210, -640]."
* **Hands Stake, Buildings Fight:** The hero claims and gathers and never deals damage. Every attack comes from something built and placed, so fights are won by layouts, not reflexes.

### Instance Litmus (Phase 4)

| Check | Question | Correct Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Math** | Is this feature about balancing what workers carry against what the build spends? | **Yes.** Throughput per worker is the metric. |
| **The Legs** | Does every resource it produces arrive with a worker? | **Yes.** Nothing enters the purse without legs. |
| **The Chores** | Does it retire a repeated action without choosing for the player? | **Yes.** Automate the verb; keep the decision. |
| **The Canvas** | Does tearing down your own building cost you materials? | **No.** Rivals may take; redesigning must not. |
| **The Ledger** | Does it make held ground count, and more ground harder to hold? | **Yes.** The map is the record; size is never a pure win. |
| **The Receipt** | If it takes something from a player, does the ledger name who, what, and where? | **Yes.** No loss without a line. |
| **The Hands** | Does it let the hero deal damage? | **No.** Hands stake; buildings fight. |

**Litmus self-test:** Five features in the build failed, and all five were cut or changed. **Photon Plates** (walls that generated wood and stone) failed The Legs and was a dominant strategy: at level one, a ten-stone wall paid for itself in twenty seconds and kept paying forever. It was cut and rebuilt as Supply Roads. **Sovereign Tax** and **Vacuum Cores** failed The Legs and became a base delivery bonus and Forward Depots. **Auto-assign** failed The Chores and became Standing Orders. The **leaderboard** failed The Ledger (a wall scored 50, an outpost 0) and now counts only held ground and Plan phases.
