## Axis: Power

**The question:** What does the player's power consist of, and how much of it do they have?

This axis is not just "how strong is the player." It is *what kind* of strength the game offers. Two games can both make the player feel powerful, one through reflexes and one through knowledge, and they will need completely different upgrade systems.

### Positions

* **Powerless, Never Helpless:** The player cannot fight the main threat, but can always hide, flee, or distract.
* **Poor:** The player can fight, but can never afford to fight everything.
* **Earned Mastery:** The character starts weak; the real power is the player's own skill, built through repeated failure.
* **Kinetic:** Power is the joy of movement itself. The point isn't to defeat things; it's how good it feels to move.
* **Toolkit:** Power is the number of ways the player can solve a problem.
* **Optimization:** Power is efficiency: builds, synergies, turns saved, the elegance of a solved system.
* **Understanding:** The only power is knowledge. A player who knows everything is all-powerful.
* **Social / Interior:** Power is persuasion, perception, and conviction.
* **Custodial:** The player doesn't embody power; they manage people who have their own wills. Power is indirect and unreliable.
* **Surplus:** Power is having more than you need. It is measured in what the player can spare, and its highest use is giving it away.

### Design Implications

* The power type decides what an upgrade means. In an Understanding game, a stat upgrade is a betrayal. In an Optimization game, it is the whole point. In an Earned Mastery game, upgrades exist but must never replace skill.
* Almost every soul in this collection rejects power fantasy through *looking cool*. Power fantasy comes from the player's own competence (Loathing states this outright).
* Custodial power needs the managed characters to be genuinely willful, or the player is just using a clumsy remote control (see components/living_world.txt).

### Tensions

* **Poor + Toolkit** turns horror into an immersive sim. That is a legitimate hybrid (*System Shock*, *Prey*), but choose it deliberately, because every new tool erodes the scarcity.
* **Understanding + any stat progression** invites the player to grind past a puzzle instead of solving it. Hearthian bans this outright.
* **Understanding of a person + a per-person score** turns knowing someone into a contest between the two people doing the knowing. Ampersand measures only the pair.
* **Surplus + a hard bottleneck** means nothing is ever spare, and the power never gets used. Gauge resolves it by limiting room rather than production: a store that fills is exactly what makes something spare.
