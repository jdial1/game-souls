## Component: Environmental Storytelling

**The principle:** The story is not told; it is found. Information is a physical object in the world, and the player assembles meaning from debris, documents, and details.

**Used by:** Looking Glass, Ashen, Hearthian, Mansion, Casebook

**Requires:** trusting_the_player (players must be willing to look; nothing points them at the story)
**Supports:** interlocking_space, shared_discovery, theme_as_mechanic
**Conflicts With:** cutscene-led delivery (the story told at the player, not found); Information: Total, where nothing is left to find

### Design Rules

* **Information as a Physical Object:** Passwords are found on discarded clipboards. The layout of an enemy base is learned by stealing a janitor's blueprints (Looking Glass). If the player wants to know something, there is a thing in the world that knows it.

* **The Story Has Already Happened:** The player arrives in the aftermath and reconstructs what happened from what was left behind.

* **Item Descriptions as Scripture:** Every item carries a few sentences of history. Individually cryptic; collectively, they tell the story of a fallen kingdom, a betrayal, and a doomed romance.
  * *Example (Ashen):* A pair of worn boots mentions a sentry who waited at a bridge for a lord who never returned. Three zones later, the player finds that bridge, and the sentry is still waiting, now hollowed and hostile.

* **Cross-Reference the World:** The meaning of one place is written in another. A mural in a drowned library depicts four constellations; a star-chart lock in a mountain observatory needs exactly four stars (Hearthian).

* **Write the Whole Truth First, Then Scatter It:** Ambiguity must hide a truth, not an absence. Write the complete hidden history before placing a single fragment, so every fragment is consistent with every other.

* **Refuse to Summarize:** Never provide a codex that explains everything. Let the community argue for years about what happened. Ambiguity is a culture generator.

### Tuning by Soul

* **Looking Glass:** Information is practical. The note tells you the safe code; the lore is a bonus.
* **Ashen:** Information is scripture. Fragmentary, mournful, and deliberately incomplete.
* **Hearthian:** Information is the progression system itself. Understanding it is the only way forward.
* **Mansion:** Information is dread. Diaries and files reveal what happened here, and what is probably still here.
* **Dwarven (variant):** The story is *generated* rather than written. See the Procedural Mythmaking pillar in souls/dwarven.txt.
* **Casebook:** Information is the frame around a puzzle. Clippings and dossiers give the player everything except the line that matters, and the proven line is written back into the record.

### What It Costs

* **Writing Discipline:** Fragments must be consistent across the entire game. A hidden history document (a "bible") is mandatory, and late plot changes ripple through every item description.
* **Missed Content:** Most players will read a fraction of what you write.

### How It Fails

* **The Lore Void** (pitfalls/lore_void.txt): Fragments are so scattered that no coherent story can be reconstructed.
* **Obscurity as a Shortcut** (pitfalls/obscurity_as_a_shortcut.txt): Designers confuse "cryptic" with "deep" and hide critical progression behind unguessable actions.
* **The Audio-Log Dump** (pitfalls/audio_log_dump.txt): Environmental storytelling becomes a pile of voice recordings that explain the plot out loud.
