## Component: Scarcity Economy

**The principle:** A tight bottleneck on a key resource turns every action into a decision. Scarcity is where tension, optimization, and meaning come from.

**Used by:** Dwarven, Loathing, Spire, Mansion, Containment, Ampersand, Casebook, Swarm, Tapestry, Assembly, Tether, Vigil

**Requires:** a deliberate topology (see Economic Topology below): a bottleneck only exists where sources and drains are deliberately unbalanced
**Supports:** legible_failure, failure_as_content, difficulty_ladder
**Conflicts With:** Inflationary topology without drains (abundance ends scarcity); synergy_engines late in a run (screen-clearing power ends the pressure); Failure: Forgone

### Design Rules

* **Poor, Not Powerless:** Give the player the means to act, but never enough to act on everything. Every encounter becomes a question: spend, save, or find another way.
  * *Example (Mansion):* Three shambling corpses block a corridor the player will cross a dozen times. The player has nine bullets. Kill them all, and there's nothing left for whatever waits upstairs. Dodge them every trip, and risk a bite every time. Neither answer is correct, and that is the point.

* **One Hard Bottleneck:** Choose the resource that defines a session and make it genuinely tight: adventures per day, stomach and liver capacity, inventory slots, energy per turn, bullets.
  * *Example (Loathing):* Each day grants a fixed number of adventures. Food and booze grant more, but stomach and liver capacity are limited. Every session becomes an optimization puzzle about what to eat, what to drink, and where to spend each turn.

* **Inventory as Anxiety:** Keep carrying capacity small and storage far away. Picking up a key means leaving behind a healing item.

* **Make Safety Cost Something:** When saving or healing is free, scarcity evaporates. Charge for it (a finite save ribbon, a rest that respawns enemies) so the player weighs "Is this worth protecting?"

* **Saying No Is a Skill:** When the player is offered more than they can use, turning things down should often be correct. A thin, focused build beats a bloated one (Spire).

* **A Hidden Floor:** Scarcity must never soft-lock the player. Quietly guarantee enough of the essentials to continue, for players who are truly out.

### Economic Topology

Every economy has a shape: where resources come from, where they go, and whether the total grows, shrinks, or only moves. The shape decides the feeling before any number is tuned, so name it first. (The vocabulary of sources, drains, converters, and feedback loops comes from Joris Dormans' Machinations; see frameworks/machinations.txt. The math here always serves the emotion.)

* **Drain (Deflationary):** Sources are capped and the game steadily takes resources away. The total only shrinks. *The feeling:* dread and careful stewardship. Every bullet spent is gone. *Souls:* Mansion, Dwarven, Tether (within a raid), Casebook (within an installment: the ration of checks and hints only shrinks, and refills only when the next installment opens).

* **Generator (Inflationary):** Sources grow with investment, and the total climbs without limit. Drains exist only to keep growth meaningful. *The feeling:* momentum and mastery. The bottleneck keeps moving because the output never stops rising. *Souls:* Assembly, Swarm, Containment, Loathing, Vigil (across a career; a Drain inside each run).

* **Zero-Sum:** Resources mostly move between players, factions, or characters rather than being created or destroyed. One side's gain is another's loss. *The feeling:* rivalry and intrigue. *Souls:* Tapestry, Tribunal (information, not goods), Tether (between raids, gear changes hands).

* **Gift Economy:** Surplus is produced to be given away, and nothing is ever taken back. *The feeling:* warmth. *Souls:* Gauge.

* **No Meaningful Economy:** The soul's core is emotional or social, and resources are incidental or absent. Don't force a topology onto it. *Souls:* Ampersand, Elysium, Hearthian, Chorus, Toybox.

**Design Rules:**

* **Name the Shape Before Tuning a Number:** Decide which topology the soul needs, then tune. A Mansion game with an Inflationary economy isn't a horror game with too much ammo; it's the wrong shape.

* **Every Generator Needs a Sink:** An economy whose sources grow must have a drain that grows with it (infinite research, a megaproject, a rising tide), or the player ends up with chests full of nothing to do.

* **Know Your Feedback Loops:** Positive feedback (the rich get richer) makes runs snowball and stories dramatic; negative feedback (the leader is slowed) keeps contests close. Choose deliberately, and say which loop produces which feeling: a snowball in Swarm is the breakpoint; a snowball in Tapestry is the blob that needs resistance.

* **Friction Is a Mechanism, Not a Leak:** Drains, attrition, and rising costs are designed, named, and visible to the player. A resource that disappears without a named sink is a bug.

### Tuning by Soul

* **Mansion:** Scarcity produces dread. Bullets, healing, and saves.
* **Loathing:** Scarcity produces optimization puzzles. Turns and consumption capacity.
* **Spire:** Scarcity produces decisions. Energy per turn, health across a run, and deck slots.
* **Dwarven:** Scarcity is emergent and indifferent. Food, materials, and sanity, and nobody tuned it for you.
* **Containment:** Scarcity is space. Money inflates away; tiles don't, and every tile is either making heat or handling it.
* **Ampersand:** Scarcity is weight. One question a day, one sealed answer, one reaction each: rare enough that each one means something, and never a budget to optimize.
* **Casebook:** Scarcity is certainty. A few checks and revealed answers per installment, shared across its pages; a check costs the same whether the guess was right or wrong.
* **Swarm:** Scarcity is time and weapon slots: a fixed run length and a strict pick at every level-up.
* **Tapestry:** Scarcity is political capital: influence, legitimacy, prestige, and the patience of the nobles who own the armies.
* **Assembly:** Scarcity is throughput, space, and the player's attention, never money.
* **Tether:** Gear Fear: the stash is the economy, and bringing good gear raises both your power and the cost of dying.
* **Vigil:** Scarcity is the seat's own pool, spent in real time against damage somebody else let through. A Drain inside a run, a Generator across the career.

### What It Costs

* **Fragile Tuning:** Too much and the tension evaporates; too little and players get stuck. Expect many rounds of economy tuning across every difficulty level.
* **Friction for Some Players:** Scarcity is stressful by design. Some players will find it unpleasant rather than exciting.

### How It Fails

* **The Soft Lock** (pitfalls/soft_lock.txt): The player saves with no resources in front of a mandatory challenge.
* **The Hoarding Trap** (pitfalls/hoarding_trap.txt): Players are so afraid of running out that they never use anything and finish the game with a full inventory.
* **Scarcity Without Choice** (pitfalls/scarcity_without_choice.txt): Resources are tight, but there's only one way to spend them.
