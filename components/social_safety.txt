## Component: Social Safety

**The principle:** A social layer between people who already know each other must never become an audience, a scoreboard, or a lever one person pulls on another. The game connects people; it never makes them owe each other.

**Used by:** Ampersand, Gauge

### Design Rules

* **Given, Never Discovered:** Groups form by invitation only: a code read aloud across a kitchen. No directory, no search, no strangers, and no names typed by anyone. Removing the surface beats moderating it, especially when the players are children.

* **No Audience, No Standings:** No feed, no leaderboard, no sharing outward, and never a list sorted by progress. The group is ordered by when people joined, and any number that exists describes the group together, never one member against another. An audience changes what people do; a ranking tells someone they're last.

* **No Levers:** No feature may let one person pull on another: no nudge button, no request for help, no read receipts on silence, no notification that names who is late or who is in need. Need may be *visible* (a thin heap, an unanswered day) but never *sent*. A request is an obligation, and obligation is where kindness turns into chores.

* **Either Can Veto, Neither Can Force:** Anything one person hides is hidden for everyone it affects, and leaving ends things for both sides. No one can make the others wait, see, or answer.

* **Asynchronous by Construction:** Nobody ever needs anybody else to be online. Whatever one person does for another lands whenever the other next looks.

* **News Is Spent Once, and Only Goes Up:** "Since you last looked" shows only what went up (a building finished, a question answered), and each piece of news is used up the first time it is actually shown. Decreases are the other person's business, not news.

* **Thanks Without Words:** Where there is no chat (and for children there shouldn't be), gratitude needs a shape the world can carry: one uncounted gesture, like a flag on a returning train, that can't itself be answered. A wave answered by a wave is a conversation.

* **The Bad-Month Test:** Every feature must still be kind when the people using it are barely speaking, or one of them has stopped playing. A missed day costs a piece, never a lecture. A broken run is rarely mentioned and never mourned. Say what is waiting, never whose fault it is.
  * *Example:* After a two-week silence, the game doesn't say "Your 41-day run ended." It says nothing about the gap, and today's question is a light one.

### Tuning by Soul

* **Ampersand:** Two people, one pair. The danger is one partner grading or pressuring the other, so every number is about the pair, and there is no witness but the other half.
* **Gauge:** A family or a few friends, including children. The danger is the family becoming a leaderboard or a debt ledger, so gifts come only from spare, need is never sent, and there is no free text anywhere.

### What It Costs

* **A Network That Doesn't Spread:** Invitation-only groups can't be topped up with strangers when they go quiet. Every player brings their own people, and the game has to be whole for a player who brings none.
* **Expression Must Be Designed:** Without chat or typed names, every kind of thanks, encouragement, and presence has to be built as something the world can carry.
* **Testing Needs Real People:** Nothing social can be seen until two real identities act. A faked partner tests a path the game doesn't have.

### How It Fails

* **The Guilt Engine:** Streaks with teeth, escalating reminders, and notifications that name a debt ("Your cousin needs wood!", "Maya is waiting"). *Symptom:* players feel they owe the group their attention, and the game becomes something they nag each other about. *Fix:* apply the Bad-Month Test; say what is waiting, never who is to blame or who needs what.
* **The Ranked Group:** Shared snapshots quietly become a scoreboard, a per-person tally appears, or a gift count becomes a ledger of who gave more. *Symptom:* someone opens the game to see where they stand. *Fix:* show changes as each person's own place growing, and any number as the group's.
* **The Nagging Feed:** News repeats until it's acknowledged. *Symptom:* good news starts to feel like a notification badge. *Fix:* news is spent the first time it is shown, and never re-announced.

### Litmus

| Check | Question | Correct Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Bad Month** | Would this feature still be kind if the people using it were barely speaking? | **Yes.** If it would sting, it's a lever. |
