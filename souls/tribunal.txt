## The "Tribunal" Game Soul

**A Framework for Asymmetric Information, Paranoia, and the Democratic Weapon**

The soul of games like *Among Us*, *Blood on the Clocktower*, *Town of Salem*, *Secret Hitler*, *Project Winter*, and the parlor games *Mafia* and *Werewolf* can be distilled into a single philosophy: "The monster looks like your friend, and the only weapon you have is a majority vote."

On the surface, these are party games, digital board games, or casual survival games. Beneath the surface, they are engines of social engineering. The game doesn't test mechanical skill; it tests the ability to lie under pressure, read people, and move a crowd. The rules are just a framework; the real puzzle is the other players in the call.

### Axis Positions

| Axis | Position |
| --- | --- |
| Failure | Terminal (for the match) |
| Information | Hidden (asymmetric) |
| Authorship | Mutual |
| Power | Social / Interior |
| Tone | Tense Paranoia |
| Structure | Runs (matches) |

### Psychological Target

What the ideal player of this soul wants from play, described with the six motivation pairs of the Quantic Foundry Gamer Motivation Model (see frameworks/gamer_motivation.txt). This is design intent, not a market segment: it names who the design serves, so that features built for someone else can be spotted.

| Motivation Pair | Level | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Action | Low | Actions are simple; the fight is rhetorical. |
| Social | High | Competition and Community at once: betray friends, then laugh about it. |
| Mastery | High | Strategy: reading people and managing a lie. |
| Achievement | Low | Little persistent progression. |
| Immersion | Low | The fiction is a thin frame for the people. |
| Creativity | Low | Expression lives in performance, not creation. |

**Design note:** Solitary or Immersion-focused players will find nothing here; the game is the other people.

### Components

* **components/match_integrity.txt:** The dead are sealed from the living, the truth comes out at the end, and the voice chat that carries the game is moderated, because the game is deception.
* **components/legible_failure.txt:** Retroactive legibility. When the match ends, every role is revealed, and every lie and clever play is laid bare for the autopsy.
* **components/shared_discovery.txt:** The match is a fast, shared investigation in which some of the investigators are polluting the evidence on purpose.
* **components/theme_as_mechanic.txt:** The theme is deception. Strip away the hidden roles, and there is no game left.
* **components/diegetic_interface.txt:** Doing a task covers the screen, blinding the player to what's happening around them. Being useful means being vulnerable.

### Signature Pillar 1: Knowledge Against Numbers

The soul is built on a balanced scale. One side has the information; the other has the numbers.

**Design Rules:**

* **The Informed Minority:** The traitors know who everyone is. They hold all the answers, but not the majority needed to win a fair fight.

* **The Ignorant Majority:** The innocents have the numbers but don't know whom to trust. Their goal is to gather information without dying.

* **The Sabotage:** The informed minority gets tools to control the flow of information (cutting the lights, locking doors, claiming false roles) to keep the majority blind.

### Signature Pillar 2: The Democratic Weapon

In a Tribunal game, players usually can't just attack a suspect. Execution is a civic process.

**Design Rules:**

* **The Vote Is the Bullet:** The only way the innocents can remove a threat is to convince a majority to vote with them. Combat is rhetorical.

* **The Cost of a Miss:** Voting out an innocent must be devastating. It doesn't just lower the innocents' numbers; it moves the traitors closer to winning. The tension is the fear of pulling the trigger on the wrong person.

* **The Talking Phase:** The game separates the action phase from the deliberation phase. The meeting is where the game is won or lost.

### Signature Pillar 3: The Alibi Engine

If the innocents stay grouped together, the traitors can never act, and the game breaks. The game must force them apart, and make what they did while apart into evidence.

**Design Rules:**

* **The Distraction Tasks:** Give innocents mandatory, mundane chores spread across the map. Tasks are an alibi engine: "I was in Electrical fixing wires" is a defense only because the game required someone to be there.

* **Every Proof Can Be Faked:** Every piece of evidence that could confirm a player's innocence needs a counterplay: a false claim, a faked task, a planted doubt. Certainty ends the game.
  * *Example:* A task that shows a visible animation to anyone watching proves the player doing it is innocent. That's why such tasks are best made optional: a setting the group can switch off when it starts to solve the game.

* **Claims Are Moves:** What a player says they saw, did, or are is a move in the game, as real as any action. Some games are built entirely on claims: a player given two bad policy cards must decide whether to tell the truth about what they were dealt.

### Signature Pillar 4: The Table Is the Game

The match doesn't end at the victory screen. It ends later, in the lobby or around the table, and the people there are the real content.

**Design Rules:**

* **The Catharsis:** After the reveal, give players room (a lobby, a table, a voice channel) to laugh, explain their bluffs, and apologize for false accusations. The match builds tension; the aftermath releases it. Without it, the paranoia lingers and players quit.

* **Roles Beyond Two Teams:** Add roles with their own goals: a jester who wins by getting voted out, a neutral who only wants to survive. They break the simple arithmetic of us and them, and make every vote more uncertain.

* **The Referee Can Be a Player:** A human host, like *Blood on the Clocktower*'s Storyteller, can shape what information each player receives to keep the game close and interesting. A game designed around a host gets pacing no algorithm can match.

### Variants & Exceptions

* *Mafia* and *Werewolf* are the parlor-game ancestors: a moderator, a night phase, a day vote, and nothing else. Every digital version adds tools to that core.
* *Among Us* adds a map, tasks, and sabotage. Its visual tasks can be turned off in the lobby settings, a practical answer to "Every Proof Can Be Faked." The dead become ghosts who can still finish tasks, so they help without speaking.
* *Blood on the Clocktower* removes the physical map and replaces the Alibi Engine with role abilities and logic puzzles. It deliberately bends "The Dead Are Sealed": dead players stay at the table, keep talking, and hold one last vote, which keeps them in the game.
* *Town of Salem* puts many roles into a text-based game of night actions and day trials, including a jester who wins by being lynched.
* *Secret Hitler* is built on claims: the president and chancellor pass policy cards to each other in secret, and each can lie about what they were dealt.
* *Project Winter* adds survival and physical combat. You can attack a suspect directly, but without the group behind you, it's usually suicide.

### What This Soul Costs

(Shared costs live in the component files: servers, anti-cheat, moderation.)

* **No Single-Player Mode:** The game is unplayable without enough people. Bots can't simulate social manipulation.
* **Group Size Is a Hard Requirement:** Matchmaking must gather a full table, and many roles only work at specific player counts.
* **New Players Get Eaten:** Experienced players read tells and exploit habits, and new players often lose without learning why. Onboarding needs to teach suspicion, not just rules.

### How It Fails

(Shared pitfalls come through the components: Dead Men Tell Tales, the toxic lobby, theme as paint. Every pitfall has its own file in pitfalls/.)

* **The Solved Meta** (pitfalls/solved_meta.txt): An exploit lets innocents confirm each other with certainty.
* **The Idle Dead** (pitfalls/idle_dead.txt): Eliminated players have nothing to do.
* **The Loudest Wins** (pitfalls/loudest_wins.txt): Discussion is dominated by whoever talks most.

### Neighboring Souls

* **Elysium:** Both make conversation the main verb of combat. Elysium's conversation is with yourself; Tribunal's is with liars.
* **Ampersand:** Both are games about other people and withheld information. Ampersand withholds so that a truth can be revealed; Tribunal withholds so that a lie can be sold.
* **Casebook:** Both are deduction. Casebook's evidence is fair and complete; Tribunal's evidence is adversarial, and some of the people handing it over are lying.

### The "Tribunal" Litmus Test

| Check | Question | Correct Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Weapon** | Can a single innocent player eliminate a suspect on their own? | **No.** Execution requires consensus. |
| **The Scale** | Does one side hold most of the information while the other holds most of the numbers? | **Yes.** The imbalance is the engine. |
| **The Fake** | Can every piece of evidence that proves innocence be faked or disputed? | **Yes.** Certainty ends the game. |
| **The Ghost** | Does an eliminated player have something to do that can't leak information to the living? | **Yes.** The dead stay in the game, and stay silent. |
| **The Autopsy** | Is the conversation after the reveal as engaging as the match itself? | **Yes.** The reveal is the payoff. |

*"You do not need a boss fight when you have eight friends in a voice call, a dead body in the cafeteria, and ten seconds left on the voting timer."*
